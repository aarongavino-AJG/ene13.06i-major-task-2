Understanding OCDLens of a student with OCD

What you don't see.

For a student with OCD, intrusive mental images can make feared possibilities feel vivid and emotionally immediate, even when no danger is visibly present. A familiar classroom or bedroom may become difficult to inhabit when these images repeatedly interrupt the moment. This experience can be understood through Judith Butler’s concept of precariousness, the shared condition of being vulnerable and dependent on circumstances and people beyond our control. Viewed through this lens, the contrast between an ordinary setting and an overwhelming internal image reveals how fragile a sense of safety can feel. The student’s perspective invites viewers to recognize this vulnerability and the importance of care when reassurance from one’s surroundings feels difficult to hold onto. For the subject, her inner struggles remain unrecognizable due to her condition’s invisible nature. Through this photo essay, we hope to use Butler’s concept of “framing” as a means of allowing others without OCD to apprehend the inner, and ever haunting precariousness that persons with OCD experience in their daily lives.

What you don't see

Through whose lens?

The photo essay presents a student’s experience with OCD through Gérard Genette’s concept of internal focalization, in which a narrative is shaped by a character’s perceptions and knowledge. By remaining centered on one student’s experience, it adopts fixed internal focalization, allowing their perspective to guide the meaning of each scene by revealing the disturbing and internal imagery she experiences. Familiar settings become emotionally charged through visual composition and captions that convey the intrusive imagery interrupting the student’s daily life. Even when the camera shows the student from outside, the narrative remains aligned with their inner experience, since focalization concerns access to consciousness rather than camera position alone. The use of double exposure as a photography technique also applies Genette’s “flashforward” concept, as a means of highlighting the anticipated dangers that the subject experiences in their mind. Together, the images and words invite viewers to understand how an outwardly ordinary moment can carry an unsettling sense of vulnerability for the person experiencing it.

About this photo journal

This project was produced as a requirement for ENE 13.06i. We do hope that through this project, readers get a deeper understanding on imagery perceptions of people with OCD, on how they perceive situations that may seem normal to others. Through this photo essay, we aim to raise awareness on OCD, a condition that is commonly quoted without a nuanced understanding of it. The narrative of this photo essay was inpsired by the experiences of those mentioned in the study of Wedge et al. (2025).

A note for our readers

For our dear readers, we have two requests. First is that we encourage you read more on mental health conditions. The first step to understanding is knowing. Second is that, we hope that the understanding comes with action, the gentle and kind one. Precarity is inevitable in the world that we live in. Our request is that we make it a tad bit better and kinder for one another.
